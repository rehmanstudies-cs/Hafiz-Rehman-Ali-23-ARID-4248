Privacy Policy

How HR Fram handles information during the prototype stage.

This Privacy Policy explains the types of information HR Fram may collect, process, store, display, transfer, or otherwise use while providing its agricultural decision-support features. It also explains the difference between the current front-end academic prototype and a future production version that may use a database, backend server, authentication system, cloud hosting, analytics, external APIs, and trained machine-learning models.

Academic Prototype Last Updated: October 2026 Pakistan-First Platform
Important prototype notice: HR Fram is currently being developed as a BSCS Final Year Project. The present version uses browser-based storage for several functions and does not yet represent a fully deployed commercial or production service. This policy is therefore intentionally written to describe both the current prototype behavior and the expected privacy responsibilities of a future backend-enabled version. It should be formally reviewed before public production deployment.

1. Scope and purpose of this Privacy Policy

HR Fram is an AI-based agricultural decision-support platform being developed to help users make more informed crop-selection and yield-planning decisions. The platform is designed around a Pakistan-first workflow and may use location, weather information, soil information, crop data, historical agricultural records, user-provided farm details, and machine-learning outputs to support its core features. This Privacy Policy applies to the HR Fram website, its crop recommendation module, yield prediction module, user dashboard, profile features, saved history, administrative interfaces, support forms, and related functionality that may be added as the project develops.

The purpose of this policy is to explain clearly what information may be involved when a user interacts with HR Fram, how that information is used, how it is currently stored during the prototype stage, when information may be sent to an external service, what choices users have, and how future versions of the system should handle data responsibly. Because the project is still under development, some parts of this policy describe intended production behavior rather than functionality that is already implemented. Where that distinction matters, the policy identifies it.

This document should be read together with the HR Fram Terms of Use. The Terms explain acceptable use, the limitations of agricultural recommendations and predictions, and the fact that HR Fram is intended to support decision-making rather than guarantee agricultural results. This Privacy Policy focuses specifically on information handling and user privacy.

2. Information HR Fram may handle

HR Fram may handle information that users provide directly, information generated through their use of the system, technical information required for specific functions, and information obtained from external services such as weather-data providers. The exact categories depend on which part of the system the user chooses to use.

2.1 Profile and account information

The user dashboard and account interfaces may handle a full name, username, email address, optional phone number, province, district or city, default farm area, preferred land-area unit, and an optional profile image. During the current front-end prototype, these values are used only to demonstrate the account experience, personalize the dashboard, and support future form pre-filling. Real account registration, identity verification, password hashing, secure session handling, and database-backed account storage are planned for the backend stage.

2.2 Authentication and recovery information

The current test environment includes a dummy login account so the complete user flow can be tested. A temporary browser-based reset token may also be created when testing the Forgot Password and Reset Password pages. These mechanisms are not intended to represent production-grade authentication. In a final deployment, passwords should never be stored in plain text, reset tokens should be generated and validated securely on the server, and authentication records should be protected using appropriate security controls.

2.3 Support information

If a user submits a Help or Support request, the system may handle the user's name, email address, selected support category, subject, message, time of submission, and an automatically generated support reference number. In the current prototype, support requests may be stored in the same browser rather than transmitted to a help-desk server. A future production version may send these requests to a backend, database, support system, or authorized project administrator.

3. Agricultural and farm-related information

The core purpose of HR Fram requires the platform to work with agricultural context. Depending on the module being used, a user may provide or generate information about province, district or city, season, farm area, crop, sowing or planting date, growth stage, expected financial year, irrigation source, fertilizer or nutrient input, approximate fertilizer quantity, seed or crop variety, and other management-related details.

Crop Recommendation may also use soil values such as Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), Potassium (K), and pH when those values are available. These inputs are optional in the current design because not every farmer has access to a recent soil test. Weather-related information may include temperature, humidity, rainfall or precipitation, and location information used to retrieve those values.

Yield Prediction may additionally store predicted yield expressed in tonnes per hectare, estimated total production for the selected farm area, a displayed uncertainty or range, crop age calculated from the sowing date, model or demo-result metadata, and the source of the prediction workflow. For example, a prediction may be started independently or may continue directly from a Crop Recommendation result.

Agricultural information entered into HR Fram should be treated as decision-support context. The system should not automatically interpret user-entered information as independently verified farm records, scientific measurements, legal land records, or certified agricultural data.

4. How HR Fram may use information

HR Fram uses information primarily to provide the features the user requests. Profile information may be used to personalize the dashboard and reduce repeated data entry. Farm and environmental information may be used to prepare crop recommendation inputs, yield prediction inputs, saved result records, printable reports, history entries, and summaries displayed to the user.

Location information may be used to retrieve relevant weather conditions. Soil information may be used as input to the crop recommendation model once the final trained model is connected. Historical yield information may be used to train, validate, or support the yield prediction model. Information collected through the administrative interface may be used to manage crop catalog entries, dataset metadata, model status information, and project-level monitoring.

During model-development work, data may also be used to evaluate whether the system is producing reasonable and technically defensible results. This may include data cleaning, feature engineering, comparing model predictions against known values, calculating performance metrics, detecting obvious input problems, and reviewing how different inputs affect prediction behavior. If user-generated data is ever incorporated into model training or evaluation in a future version, the project should clearly document that practice before doing so rather than silently reusing personal or farm information.

HR Fram should not use information for unrelated advertising, behavioral profiling, sale to data brokers, or other purposes that are unrelated to the project without first updating the policy and, where appropriate, obtaining meaningful user consent.

5. Browser storage in the current prototype

The present front-end implementation uses browser localStorage for several functions. This may include profile details, a test login session, the dummy account configuration, saved crop recommendation records, saved yield prediction records, temporary recommendation-to-yield transfer data, password-reset demo tokens, support requests, administrative crop records, administrative dataset metadata, and activity logs created while testing the website.

localStorage is specific to the browser and device being used. Information stored there does not automatically synchronize across computers, phones, browsers, or private-browsing sessions. If a user clears browser storage, uses a different browser, changes device, or uses certain privacy settings, that information may no longer be available.

Browser storage is useful for demonstrating the user experience before a backend exists, but it is not a substitute for a secure production database. Sensitive production information should not be stored in plain browser storage merely because it is convenient. When the backend is implemented, HR Fram should move account authentication, server-managed sessions, secure password handling, user records, support tickets, administrative permissions, and other appropriate data into controlled server-side systems.

6. Weather information and external services

HR Fram is designed to reduce unnecessary manual input by retrieving weather information automatically when the user selects an appropriate location. To perform this function, the application may send a location name or geographic coordinates to an external weather or geocoding service. The service may return values such as temperature, relative humidity, precipitation, latitude, longitude, administrative area, and related weather metadata.

When an external service is used, that provider operates under its own technical systems and privacy practices. HR Fram cannot control the provider's infrastructure. Before final deployment, the project should document the production weather provider being used, review the provider's applicable terms and privacy information, and ensure that the integration sends only the information needed for the requested function.

The system should avoid sending unrelated profile information to a weather service. A weather request generally needs location context, not a user's name, phone number, profile image, password, or complete farm-management record. Maintaining that separation reduces unnecessary data exposure.

7. Machine learning, model inputs, and generated results

HR Fram is intended to use machine-learning techniques for crop recommendation and yield prediction. Machine-learning systems operate on data and may produce outputs that contain uncertainty. The presence of artificial intelligence does not make every result correct, complete, or suitable for every farm.

The current interface still contains demo logic in places where final models have not yet been connected. Those demo outputs are identified as such. Once trained models are integrated, the project should maintain clear distinctions between observed data, user-provided data, external API data, model predictions, calculated values, and explanatory text.

Model-development datasets may include public agricultural datasets, district-level crop production and yield information, environmental data, soil-related data, and other authorized sources. The project should maintain records of dataset origin, relevant licenses or usage conditions, preprocessing steps, model versions, training dates, and evaluation results. If a dataset contains personal or identifiable information, additional privacy review may be required before it is used.

HR Fram should not claim that a model uses a specific feature unless that feature is actually present in appropriate training data or has been incorporated using a defensible methodology. For example, collecting irrigation, fertilizer, variety, sowing date, or soil values in the interface does not automatically prove that those inputs affect a model. The final system should accurately describe which inputs are genuinely used by each trained model.

8. User accounts, sessions, and administrative access

A production version of HR Fram may allow users to create individual accounts so recommendations, predictions, profile details, and saved history remain available across sessions and devices. Account systems should use appropriate password protection, session management, authorization checks, and database controls. Users should not be able to access another user's private results simply by changing a URL or browser parameter.

Administrative access should be restricted to authorized project administrators. The admin panel may eventually allow authorized personnel to manage datasets, crop records, model information, system users, support requests, and monitoring data. Administrative permissions create additional privacy and security responsibilities, so production access should be based on authenticated roles rather than a publicly accessible page.

The current prototype may show local demo-user information inside the admin interface because both pages use the same browser storage. This behavior is only for demonstration and does not represent a secure multi-user administration system.

9. Information sharing and disclosure

HR Fram should limit information sharing to what is necessary for operation, development, security, and authorized project administration. During the current local prototype, most information remains in the user's browser unless a feature such as automatic weather retrieval sends a request to an external service.

A future hosted version may use service providers for web hosting, databases, authentication, email delivery, backups, monitoring, weather information, or other infrastructure. If these providers process user information on behalf of HR Fram, the production privacy documentation should identify relevant categories of providers and explain the purpose of the processing.

HR Fram should not sell personal information. It should also avoid disclosing personal or farm-related information publicly unless the user intentionally chooses a public-sharing feature and understands what will be visible. Result-sharing links should be designed carefully because a link may contain or provide access to farm information, prediction values, and location context.

Information may also need to be disclosed if required by applicable law, legal process, or a valid request from an authorized public authority. Any such production process should be handled carefully and limited to what is legally required.

10. Data retention and deletion

During the prototype stage, browser-stored information may remain until it is overwritten, manually removed by the application, cleared through browser settings, or lost when browser data is reset. Because there is no production backend account database yet, the project does not currently provide a centralized server-side retention schedule.

Before production deployment, HR Fram should define how long account records, recommendation history, prediction history, support requests, administrative logs, model-related records, and backups are retained. Retention should be based on legitimate project needs rather than storing everything indefinitely.

A future account system should provide appropriate ways for users to correct or remove information where technically and legally appropriate. Backup systems may require a limited period before deleted information is fully removed from all backup copies.

11. Security responsibilities and limitations

HR Fram should use reasonable technical and organizational safeguards appropriate to the sensitivity of the information being handled. Production controls may include secure password hashing, HTTPS encryption, server-side authorization, database access controls, input validation, file-upload restrictions, logging, secure backups, dependency updates, and restrictions on administrative functions.

No website or information system can guarantee absolute security. Users should protect their account credentials and avoid sharing passwords. They should also be careful when using public or shared devices. If a user remains logged in on a shared computer, another person may be able to access information that appears in the dashboard.

The current prototype should not be interpreted as demonstrating all production security controls. Browser-based dummy authentication exists to test the website flow, not to provide real security.

12. User choices and control over information

Users should have meaningful control over the information they provide. In the current prototype, many fields are optional, including profile photo, phone number, soil-test values, irrigation details, fertilizer information, and seed variety. Users can choose whether to provide optional information based on the feature they are using.

Users may edit profile information through the dashboard. Optional profile photos can be removed. Browser storage can also be cleared through browser settings, although doing so may remove saved results, preferences, test account data, and other locally stored information.

In a future production system, users should be able to review and correct important account information. Depending on applicable requirements and the final architecture, the system may also need mechanisms for account deletion, data export, consent management, communication preferences, or other privacy controls.

13. Children and educational use

HR Fram is being developed as an agricultural and academic decision-support project and is not specifically designed as a service for children. If the project is later used in educational environments involving minors, additional review may be required regarding account creation, consent, data collection, and institutional supervision.

The platform should avoid intentionally collecting unnecessary personal information from minors and should be configured appropriately for the environment in which it is deployed.

14. Changes to this Privacy Policy

HR Fram will continue to change as the Final Year Project progresses. Features that are currently browser-only may later use Flask or another backend framework, MySQL or another database, cloud hosting, production authentication, email delivery, machine-learning APIs, improved weather integration, and model monitoring. Any material change to how personal or agricultural information is collected, stored, shared, or retained should be reflected in an updated Privacy Policy.

The date shown near the top of this page should be updated whenever significant privacy-related changes are made. If HR Fram becomes a publicly deployed service, users should be given appropriate notice of major changes rather than relying only on a silent update to this page.

15. Privacy questions and contact

Questions about this Privacy Policy, the handling of information, or the operation of the current prototype can be submitted through the HR Fram Help & Support page. During the current development stage, support requests may remain in browser storage until the backend support workflow is implemented.

Before public deployment, the project should publish an appropriate official contact method for privacy and account-related requests and define how those requests will be received, verified, assigned, and resolved.

HR Fram is an academic agricultural decision-support project. This Privacy Policy is intended to provide transparency about the prototype and to establish a responsible foundation for the final system. It does not replace formal legal review that may be required before public or commercial deployment.
